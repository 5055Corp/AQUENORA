How to build a mobile app you can sell in 12 months on less than $500

What the 2025–26 data says about where app money goes, which platform suits a first-time builder, how buyers value small apps, and a starter project with code you can run.

Key takeaways
  1. The money is real, and it's now mostly in non-game apps. People spent $167B in app stores in 2025, and for the first time non-game apps earned more than games [1].
  2. Most apps earn very little, so the odds are against you. New subscription app launches rose roughly 7× after AI coding tools arrived. A typical new app earns about $72 a month after one year, and only about 4.6% reach $10K a month within two years [3][4][5].
  3. Recommended path: an iOS-first subscription app built with Expo (React Native) that solves one narrow problem. iPhone users account for about 70% of app store spending, and one codebase also gives you an Android version [2].
  4. Small apps sell for about 2–5× their yearly profit. On Flippa in the first half of 2026, apps sold for about 2.6× annual profit on average and about 5.5× in the top quartile [8].
  5. Selling in 6–12 months is ambitious. If you keep that timeline, plan to sell a small, proven asset rather than a big business, and launch by month 3.

Sensor Tower's State of Mobile 2026 found that consumers spent $167B on in-app purchases in 2025, and that generative AI apps helped non-game apps out-earn games for the first time [1]. Downloads barely grew, so the growth comes from charging existing users more, not from new users. One sign of change: Sensor Tower reported that in Q2 2026, revenue grew faster on Google Play (+10%) than on the App Store (+3%) [19]. iOS still dominates in absolute dollars, and about 77% of new subscription apps now launch on iOS [18].

The supply shock: why most new apps earn little

RevenueCat's State of Subscription Apps 2026 covers 115,000 apps and $16B in revenue. It describes a "winner-take-more" market: the top 25% of apps grew 80% year on year, while the bottom 25% shrank by 33% [3]. AI coding tools pushed new subscription app launches from about 2,000 a month in January 2022 to about 14,700 in January 2026 [4].

What earns money

  • Utility, productivity and health apps. Adapty's 2026 report finds utilities, entertainment and productivity lead subscription revenue. Utility apps also have the highest 12-month value per trial user ($68.90) and the best first-renewal retention (58.1%) [7].
  • Asking for payment upfront converts better. Apps that require payment before use convert about 5× better than freemium apps (10.7% vs. lower). Free trials of 17+ days convert 70% better than short ones [3].
  • Weekly plans earn the most. Weekly subscriptions now generate 55.5% of subscription revenue, and weekly-plus-trial is the highest-value paywall setup Adapty measured [7].
  • AI apps earn more per customer but lose customers faster. AI apps earn 41% more per paying user but churn 30% faster [6]. That trade-off matters to buyers, who pay for retention.

Verdict. Go with mobile. It has the best evidence that people pay, the clearest buyer market and the lowest beginner barrier with Expo. You also chose mobile, so the starter project in section 6 is built for it. A Shopify app is a strong second choice once you can code, because merchants pay more per month. How to pick your idea (my inference from the data)
  • Pick something people use every week for one clear job, such as a utility, tracker or checklist. Utilities lead on retention [7].
  • Serve a narrow group you understand, like one hobby, trade or health routine, instead of competing with big generalist apps.
  • Choose something you can build in about 6 weeks with no server: data stored on the phone, and payments through RevenueCat.
  • Read 1–3 star reviews of the top apps in your niche. Repeated complaints show what to build.

3. What your app could sell for

Selling the app

Buyers of small apps mostly value them as a multiple of annual profit. Reported figures vary by marketplace:

  • Flippa, first half of 2026: about 2.6× annual profit on average, about 5.5× in the top quartile [8][10]
  • Acquire.com, 2025 deals under $100K net income: 3.7× average [9]
  • Selling fees: about 10% on Flippa and about 8% on Acquire.com, plus listing costs [9]
Worked example (illustration, not a valuation)
500 subscribers × $5.99/mo = $2,995 revenue/mo
− ~15% store fee − ~$150 in tools = ~$2,400 profit/mo → ~$28,800/yr
× 2.6 (average) = ~$75,000  ·  × 5.5 (top quartile) = ~$158,000
− 8–10% selling fee

About your 6–12 month timeline: buyers reward a steady track record and good retention. A few months of revenue counts as limited evidence and usually gets a lower multiple (my inference; not separately sourced). To keep that timeline, launch by month 3 and keep clean records from day one.

5. 12-month plan

At 5–15 hours a week
Weeks 1–3
Learn and check demand. Do a free JavaScript and React basics course. Shortlist 3 ideas, read competitor reviews and pick one. Write down the single job the app does.
Weeks 4–10
Build the first version. Use the starter project below: core feature, data saved on the phone, paywall. Test it with 5–10 real people through TestFlight.
Month 3
Launch. Enrol in Apple's Small Business Program before your first sale, since enrolment isn't automatic [11]. Ship to the App Store first, then Google Play.
Months 4–9
Grow. Improve your App Store listing (title, screenshots, keywords) and test paywall prices and trial lengths. Post where your niche gathers online. Release a meaningful update every month.
Months 9–12
Prepare the sale. Export revenue and retention charts from RevenueCat. Write a short handover document: how to build it, which accounts it uses, monthly costs. Get valuations, then list it on Flippa or Acquire.com.

7. Pre-sale checklist

Set up from day one
  • Create dedicated accounts for the app (email, developer accounts, RevenueCat, domain) so you can hand them over cleanly.
  • Keep the code in a private GitHub repository from the first commit.
  • Track monthly revenue, profit, active subscribers and churn. Buyers price apps on profit [10].
  • Keep monthly costs low and documented. Every dollar of cost is roughly 2.6–5.5 dollars off the sale price [8].
  • Avoid depending on yourself: no manual tasks, no custom server only you understand.
